What this book is

A hands-on guide for Indian Chartered Accountants, articled assistants and finance teams on using Claude Code across direct tax, GST, audit, internal audit, reporting, advisory, compliance and forensic work, with worked examples, three tested walkthroughs on practice files, copy-ready prompts and a careful routine for client data.

It follows one fictional Pune practice through a year of learning to use Claude Code, Anthropic's AI tool that works on the files on your own computer, on Indian work: Tally and ERP exports, 26AS and AIS, GSTR-2B, CARO, Form 3CD, Schedule III and the ICAI Code of Ethics. The approach throughout: the CA leads, the AI does the mechanical part, and the CA checks and signs.

  • Client data, handled with care: what leaves your laptop, what doesn't, and a routine for your team.
  • Checking AI output before it reaches a client or a signature: control totals, re-performance, and the replies that should make you push back.
  • Limits: made-up sections and case law, stale thresholds, misread scans, arithmetic done in its head, and the jobs you shouldn't give it.
  • Try it yourself: GSTR-2B reconciliation, a TDS exception scan for tax audit, and internal-audit sampling, on free practice files with planted traps and answer keys.

Direct Tax: AIS against the books

In this folder I have `AIS_FY2025-26.json` and `Tally_Ledgers_FY2025-26.xlsx` for a client.
Do not open either file in full. Read only the structure of the JSON and the headers and first 5 rows of each Excel sheet.
Then write a Python script `ais_recon.py` that runs on this computer and:
1. Flattens the AIS into one table: information category, source name, TAN or PAN of source, date, amount, TDS.
2. Matches each AIS row to the ledger by TAN/PAN where available, then by party name, amount (within ₹10) and date (within 7 days).
3. Writes `AIS_Recon.xlsx` with sheets: Matched, In AIS not in books, In books not in AIS, Summary by category.
4. In the Summary, show for each category: AIS total, books total, difference.
Run the script and show me only the Summary sheet and the count of rows on each sheet.

Indirect Tax: GST: GSTR-2B against the purchase register

In this folder: `GSTR2B_Jul.xlsx`, `GSTR2B_Aug.xlsx`, `GSTR2B_Sep.xlsx` (portal downloads) and `Purchase_Register_Q2.xlsx`. Do not read the files in full. Look at the sheet names, headers and first 5 rows of the B2B sheet in one 2B file and of the purchase register.
Then write a Python script `recon_2b.py` that runs on this computer and:
1. Combines the B2B and B2B-CDNR sheets of the three 2B files into one table.
2. Builds a match key: GSTIN + invoice number cleaned (remove the financial year in any format, remove letters and symbols, remove leading zeros) + taxable value rounded to the rupee.
3. Matches in rounds: exact invoice number, then match key, then GSTIN + taxable value within 30 days. Record the round in a column called Match Basis.
4. Treats a tax difference of up to ₹5 per invoice as matched, and flags anything larger.
Write `ITC_Recon_Q2.xlsx` with sheets: Matched, In 2B not in books, In books not in 2B, Amount differences, Summary by month and tax head. Run it and show me only the Summary and the row count of each sheet.

Statutory Audit: General ledger scrutiny that produces an exception workbook

I am auditing the general ledger in GL_FY2025-26.xlsx. It is client data.
Do not open or print the data rows. Read only the column headers, then write a Python script gl_scrutiny.py that runs on this computer and does the following on every row:
1. Round amounts: debit or credit of ₹1,00,000 or more that is an exact multiple of ₹10,000.
2. Postings dated on a Sunday, or on any date in Holidays_FY26.xlsx.
3. Entries dated 25 March 2026 to 31 March 2026, and 1 April 2026 to 10 April 2026 if present.
4. Narration blank, or containing: adjustment, reversal, suspense, as per MD, cash, misc.
5. Ledgers with fewer than 5 entries in the whole year.
Write GL_Exceptions.xlsx with: a Summary sheet (test, rule used, count, total value);
one sheet per test with Date, Voucher Type, Voucher No, Ledger, Debit, Credit, Narration;
a Combined sheet listing each voucher once with the number of tests it hit, highest first;
and a Parameters sheet recording the file name, row count, debit and credit totals, and the rules above. Show me only the Summary counts on screen. Ask before running anything.

Internal Audit and Controls: Drafting a risk control matrix from a process narrative

Read P2P_Narrative.docx. Draft a risk control matrix in RCM_P2P_Draft.xlsx with columns:
Sub-process, Risk ID, Risk Description, Control Objective, Control ID, Control Description, Control Owner (role), Frequency, Preventive/Detective, Manual/Automated/IT-dependent, Key Control (Y/N), Evidence of Operation, Suggested Test of Design, Suggested Test of Operation, Source (quote the sentence from the narrative), Gap or Query.
Cover these sub-processes: vendor master, purchase requisition, PO, goods receipt, invoice booking, payment. Use only what the narrative says. Where a risk exists but the narrative describes no control, add the row, leave Control Description blank and write the gap in Gap or Query. Do not invent controls. Then list your top 5 questions for the process owner.

Forensic and Investigations: A duplicate payment sweep

Both files are client data. Do not open or print any rows; read only the headers.
Write a Python script dup_payments.py that runs on this computer and finds:
A. Exact duplicates: same vendor code, same invoice number, same amount.
B. Same vendor and same amount, paid within 30 days of each other, different invoice numbers.
C. Near-duplicate invoice numbers for the same vendor: compare after removing spaces, hyphens, slashes, leading zeros and prefixes like INV; also flag numbers that differ by one character (for example 0 and O, 1 and I) where the amounts match.
D. The same invoice number and amount paid to two different vendor codes.
E. Different vendor codes in Vendor_Master.xlsx sharing a bank account number.
Write Duplicate_Sweep.xlsx with a Summary sheet (test, groups, rows, value at risk), one sheet per test where related payments share a Group No, and a Parameters sheet (files, row counts, total paid, the rules and windows used). Keep the 30-day window as a setting. Show me only the Summary on screen.

Frequently asked questions

Who is AI for CAs written for?

Practising Chartered Accountants, partners and managers in small and mid-size practices, articled assistants, CA students and in-house finance teams in India.

Do I need to know coding or Python?

No. You describe the job in plain English and Claude Code writes and runs the script on your own laptop. Chapter 2 sets it up on Windows in about 15 minutes. Your part is the CA's part: read each permission prompt, check the control totals and re-perform a few items by hand.

Is it safe to use AI on client data?

Chapter 1 covers this in detail. Whatever Claude Code reads or prints is sent to Anthropic; files it never opens are not. The book's main habit is to have the AI write a script that reads the client file on your laptop, so the AI sees headers, a few rows and the totals rather than the full data. That sharply reduces what is sent, but it is a habit, not a guarantee, so the book also gives a clean-folder routine, privacy settings by plan and a one-page client-data checklist.

Direct tax, GST, statutory audit, internal audit and controls, accounting and reporting, advisory and FP&A, corporate law and compliance, and forensic work; checking AI output; building a practice playbook; the limits of AI on Indian tax and audit; and three tested walkthroughs on practice files: a GSTR-2B reconciliation, a TDS exception scan for the tax audit, and internal-audit sampling of vendor payments.

What is Claude Code, and what does it cost?

Claude Code is Anthropic's AI tool that works on the files on your own computer. It needs a paid Claude plan; the free plan does not include it. Prices and plans change, so check Anthropic's current pricing. The book is independent and not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic.

Which version of Indian tax law does it follow?

Worked examples use FY 2025-26 under the Income-tax Act, 1961, after the Finance Act, 2025, as checked on 31 October 2026. The book flags that the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, and it tells you to check every threshold, rate and due date against current law.
